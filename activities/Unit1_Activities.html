What we do today — 170 minutes, 7 phases, no slides

Session Activities
Core message · Unit 1
An idea becomes an opportunity only when it meets a real need with means you actually control.
  • Ideas are abundant, and generating them is now effectively free.
  • What is scarce is the judgement that separates an idea from an opportunity for this team, at this moment, with these means.
  • Screening, not generating, is the entrepreneurial skill this unit builds.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
1Systematically analyse current and future business opportunities in terms of entrepreneurial behaviours and capability creation.
3Analyse the stages of the entrepreneurial cycle.
Core message distilled after Bostelaar (2024), Clarify Your Core Message: decompose, prioritise, three sentences, one sentence.

Driving question
Is an opportunity discovered, or is it made?
1
The Provocation
10 min

Your facilitator opens with a single claim: an opportunity is not found, it is constructed.

Your task: listen, then sit in silence for two minutes. Do not write, do not discuss.

Two or three of you will then be asked what came to mind. The discomfort of having no framework ready is the point of the exercise.

2
Gallery Exploration
25 min

Open the Unit 1 Framework Gallery on your device. It contains ten frameworks across Slibraries 1 and 2.

Work in pairs. Find the three frameworks that would most help a founder decide whether an idea is worth pursuing.

Write down for each: framework name, Slibrary number, and one sentence on why you chose it. Open the A4 reference sheet for at least one of them.

3
Idea Generation Round
25 min

Individually: write five venture ideas in five minutes. Quantity, not quality.

Then run the AI divergence step — use one tool to expand each idea into three variants. Keep the prompts you used.

Rule for the whole course, established here: AI drafts, you decide. Nothing the tool produced is yours until you have judged it.

4
NABC Screening
30 min

In teams of three, take the strongest four ideas in the group and screen each one against the NABC Idea Screen.

Need, Approach, Benefit per cost, Competition — including the customer doing nothing.

Kill at least two ideas. A team that kills nothing has not screened; it has admired.

Break · 10 minutes
5
Effectuation Switch
25 min

Take your surviving idea and rewrite it twice: once causally (goal first, means selected) and once effectually (means first, goals emergent).

List your three means: who you are, what you know, whom you know. State the loss you could afford on the next step.

Which version is more defensible given what you actually know today?

6
Two-Minute Pitches
30 min

Each team pitches its screened opportunity in two minutes, using one framework from the gallery explicitly by name.

The room's only question after each pitch: what evidence would make you abandon this?

Facilitator records the answers — they become the discovery plan for Unit 2.

7
Reflection & Personal Slibrary
15 min

Note the three frameworks you will carry forward from today into Assessment 1.

Start your personal Slibrary: one page per framework, in your own words, with the source.

Complete the Unit 1 Reflection Prompt before the next session.

What to bring

  • Laptop or phone for the framework gallery
  • One AI tool of your choice, with prompt history visible
  • Printed NABC screen (one per team)
  • Sticky notes and markers
Deliverable by the end of the session: One screened opportunity per team, with an NABC page and a written list of the evidence that would disprove it.
