Core message · Unit 3
A working product is not a business; the model is how the value you create comes back as money.
  • A business model states who pays, for what, on what schedule, and at what cost to serve them.
  • Every block of it is a hypothesis until evidence is attached to it.
  • Unit economics, not enthusiasm, decide whether growth creates or destroys value.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
1Systematically analyse current and future business opportunities in terms of entrepreneurial behaviours and capability creation.
3Analyse the stages of the entrepreneurial cycle.
Core message distilled after Bostelaar (2024), Clarify Your Core Message: decompose, prioritise, three sentences, one sentence.

Driving question
Who pays, for what, and why does the money keep coming?
1
The Revenue Question
10 min

The facilitator asks one question of each team: who pays you, and when exactly does the money arrive?

Teams that answer with a customer segment rather than a payment mechanism will notice the gap immediately.

Hold that gap; the session closes it.

2
Gallery Exploration
20 min

Open the Unit 3 Framework Gallery: Slibraries 5 and 6.

Read the A4 sheets for the Business Model Canvas and the Value Proposition Canvas before drawing anything.

Note the distinction that matters today: the canvas describes; it does not test.

3
Canvas Build
30 min

Draw all nine blocks on one sheet, in bullet form. No paragraphs.

Then read the canvas aloud as a single sentence: for this segment, we solve this, delivered this way, paid for like this, at this cost.

Where the sentence breaks, the model breaks.

4
Value Proposition Fit
30 min

Build the customer profile from your Unit 2 interview evidence only — jobs, pains, gains, ranked by importance.

Map relievers and creators against the top-ranked items. Delete anything that addresses a low-ranked pain.

Mark, in red, every claim of fit for which you have no evidence.

Break · 10 minutes
5
Money Mechanics
30 min

Choose your revenue pattern from the taxonomy and state its consequences: cash timing, churn exposure, forecasting method.

Compute a first pass at unit economics: cost to acquire one customer, contribution margin per customer, months to payback.

If your LTV:CAC is below three, that is a business model problem, not a marketing problem.

6
The AI Block
25 min

Where does AI sit in your model — value proposition, key activity, key resource, or cost structure? Place it explicitly.

Cost the AI Iceberg: licences, data work, integration, monitoring, human review. Any layer at zero requires a written reason.

If your defensibility claim is a data flywheel, name the user action that generates the data and the improvement the user sees.

7
Canvas Wall Critique
15 min

Canvases go on the wall. Every team writes one question on another team's canvas: the question they cannot answer.

Take the questions home; they are your Assessment 1 gaps.

Complete the Unit 3 Reflection Prompt before the next session.

What to bring

  • A3 Business Model Canvas and Value Proposition Canvas (Strategyzer templates)
  • Unit 2 interview evidence
  • Spreadsheet for the first unit economics pass
  • Red marker
Deliverable by the end of the session: One completed canvas per team, a value proposition fit map marked for evidence, and a first unit-economics calculation.
