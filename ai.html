The approved course outline for MENTR101 predates the general availability of generative AI in venture practice. This track is the integration: each unit carries a second Slibrary that teaches AI at the point in the entrepreneurial cycle where students will actually use it, together with the governance and integrity requirements that come with it. Nothing in the original outline is removed; the AI content is added to the existing unit structure.

The four standing rules of AI use in this course

  1. AI drafts, the entrepreneur decides. Generative tools expand the option space. Selection, judgement and responsibility remain human, and remain yours.
  2. Nothing is a fact until it is verified. Every figure, quotation, source and citation produced by an AI tool is checked against its primary source before it enters a submission. AI is never cited as the authority for a fact.
  3. Declare the use. Every submission carries an AI declaration: which tool, for which task, and how the output was revised. Undeclared use is an academic integrity breach.
  4. Protect what is not yours to share. Confidential, commercially sensitive and personal data never enters a public AI service. Under the GDPR a lawful basis is required before any personal data is processed at all.

The AI declaration required with every submission

Reproduce this block at the end of the assessment document, after the reference list:

  • Tools used: name and version of each AI system used.
  • Tasks supported: for example idea generation, structuring, language editing, code, data analysis.
  • Extent of use: what proportion of the text is AI-drafted and how it was revised.
  • Verification: how the factual claims and sources were checked.
  • Declaration: a signed statement that the analysis, argument and conclusions are the student's own.

What AI will not do for you in this course

It will not conduct your customer interviews, it will not make your assumptions defensible, and it will not answer the questions asked in the pitch. Assessment 2 includes a question-and-answer session precisely because the ability to defend a plan without the tool in the room is the competence being examined.
