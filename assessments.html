Both assessments follow the approved course outline. Assessment 1 is a written project; Assessment 2 is a report and presentation with a question-and-answer session. Harvard referencing is required in both, with in-text citations and a bibliography — one does not substitute for the other.

Assessment 1 — Business Model Canvas and Market Validation Report (40%)

Objective

Evaluate your ability to create a viable business model and to validate the business idea through market research. Due in Week 5. Submitted as a single PDF with a cover page giving your name, student ID, course title and assessment title.

ComponentWeightWhat is required
Business Model Canvas20%All nine blocks completed for your chosen idea: value proposition, customer segments, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure. Clear, concise, bullet form with short descriptions. Use the Strategyzer template or a recognised BMC tool.
Market Validation Report20%Two to three pages: market analysis (target market, customer needs, trends), competitive analysis (direct and indirect competitors, advantages, market gaps), validation technique used (surveys, interviews or prototype testing) and the actionable insights that follow. Charts and visuals where they add evidence.

Assessment criteria

CriterionWeightWhat earns the marks
Clarity and completeness of the BMC7.5%Clear definitions and logical connections between the nine components.
Market analysis5%Depth of research, understanding of the market, credible analysis of trends.
Competitive analysis3%Comprehensive competitor assessment and identification of genuine market gaps.
Validation insights4.5%Relevance and effectiveness of the validation technique and the quality of the insights drawn.

Assessment 2 — Comprehensive Business Plan and Pitch Presentation (60%)

Objective

Assess your ability to develop a detailed business plan and to pitch it effectively. Due in Week 10, split evenly between the written plan (30%) and the pitch (30%). Presentations are scheduled in class time in Week 10.

ComponentWeightWhat is required
Comprehensive Business Plan30%Ten to fifteen pages with visuals: executive summary, business description, market analysis, marketing strategy, operational plan, management and organisation, financial plan (income statement, cash flow, balance sheet) and risk analysis. Professional business plan format with clear headings. Submitted as PDF.
Pitch Presentation30%A ten-minute pitch, ten to twelve slides: introduction, problem and solution, market opportunity and validation, business model, marketing and sales strategy, financial overview and funding requirement, and a closing call to action. Slides submitted as PowerPoint or PDF.

Assessment criteria

CriterionWeightWhat earns the marks
Executive summary5%Clear and compelling summary of the business, written last and structured answer-first.
Business description5%Detailed and well-explained concept, products and business model.
Market analysis10%Comprehensive, evidenced and insightful, with sources that survive scrutiny.
Marketing strategy10%Effective and realistic plan covering branding, pricing, channels and sales approach.
Operational plan5%Feasible and well-structured operations, key activities and process flow.
Financial plan5%Accurate, internally consistent and realistic projections with stated assumptions.
Pitch delivery10%Clarity, engagement, timing, eye contact and the quality of answers in the Q&A.
Pitch content10%Coherence, relevance and quality of the argument presented.

AI use in both assessments

AI support is permitted and expected to be used well. It must be declared. Attach the AI declaration after the reference list, stating tools, tasks, extent of use, and how factual claims were verified. Unverified AI output presented as evidence, fabricated citations, and undeclared AI-drafted text are academic integrity matters, not style issues. The full protocol is on the AI in the Cycle page.

Referencing and academic formalities

  • Harvard referencing style is required in all written work, with consistent in-text citations and a bibliography. One cannot substitute for the other.
  • Literature searches use academic databases such as Google Scholar or EBSCO. Keyword selection determines what you find.
  • Academic sources take priority over general web content, particularly for theories, models and frameworks.
  • Incomplete referencing may result in lower marks or academic penalties; referencing forms part of the rubric.
