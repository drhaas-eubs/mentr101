Joseph A. Schumpeter

1883-1950 · Creative Destruction
Author 01
of 35

Why this work exists

Schumpeter moved the entrepreneur from the margin of economics to its centre, arguing that growth comes from disruption rather than from equilibrium.

Foundation

Austrian political economy, written against the neoclassical assumption that markets tend towards a stable equilibrium.

How it is used in this course

Explains why an idea must create a new combination, not a marginal improvement, and why every advantage is time-limited.

Framework map

The frameworks this author contributes to the MENTR101 galleries.

Frameworks

Creative destructionNew combinationsEntrepreneurial profit as temporary rent

Limitations

Describes systems, not individual behaviour; offers no method for finding the next combination.

Enduring impact

The intellectual origin of disruption theory, from Christensen to platform competition.

Key reference (Harvard referencing style)

Schumpeter, J.A. (1942) Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy. New York: Harper & Brothers.
