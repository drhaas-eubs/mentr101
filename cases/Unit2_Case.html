Tavolo: The Interviews That Said Yes

A Milan venture with forty enthusiastic conversations and no customers
©️ Dr. Hildegard Haas · Genedia AG Education
Case Study

Tavolo is a two-person venture in Milan building a reservation and no-show management tool for independent restaurants. The founders, Giulia and Marco, spent six weeks interviewing restaurant owners. They spoke to forty-one. Thirty-six said the product sounded useful. Twenty-two said they would probably use it. Nine said they would definitely pay.

The founders built for four months on the strength of those conversations. At launch they contacted all forty-one restaurants. Four installed the free trial. One converted to the €39 monthly plan, and cancelled after seven weeks.

Reviewing the interview notes afterwards, Giulia noticed a pattern. Almost every conversation had opened the same way: she had described the product first and then asked whether it would be useful. The questions that followed were about the future — would you use this, how much would you pay, would you recommend it. Nobody had been asked what they did last Saturday when four tables did not show up.

Marco had also run the market analysis through an AI assistant, which reported that Italian restaurants lose an average of eleven per cent of covers to no-shows and that the segment spends €340 million annually on digital tools. Neither figure could be traced when a potential investor asked for the source. One appeared to originate in a vendor blog post; the other could not be found at all.

The competitive picture had been treated equally quickly. Tavolo's deck listed three named competitors, all of them software companies. It did not list the paper diary, the WhatsApp group, or the owner's memory — which is what thirty-eight of the forty-one restaurants were actually using.

Giulia and Marco have four months of runway and one strong asset: forty-one restaurant owners who take their calls.

Your task

  1. Rewrite five of Tavolo's interview questions to satisfy The Mom Test. What would each new question have revealed?
  2. Using Jobs to Be Done, state the job restaurant owners are hiring their current solution to do. Why did Tavolo not compete for it?
  3. Apply the Source Triangulation Check to the two market figures. What should appear in the deck instead?
  4. Redraw the competitive analysis with the real alternatives included. What does the positioning map look like now?
  5. The founders have four months and forty-one warm contacts. Design their next two weeks of customer discovery.
Bring written answers to questions 1 and 2; the remaining questions are worked in the session.

Tavolo is a teaching case constructed for MENTR101. The situation is composite; no real company is described.
