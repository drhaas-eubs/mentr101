Vera Health: The Plan Nobody Could Defend

A digital physiotherapy venture meets its first serious investor questions
©️ Dr. Hildegard Haas · Genedia AG Education
Case Study
A plan is worth what its weakest assumption can withstand.Core message after Bostelaar · Intended learning outcomes 3, 4

Vera Health is an eighteen-month-old venture in Vienna offering guided physiotherapy programmes through an app, sold to employers as a benefit. It has 3,100 active users across eleven corporate clients, a clinical advisory board, and a plan that reads well.

The plan projects €4.8 million of revenue in year three, based on a stated fourteen per cent share of the Austrian and German corporate wellbeing market. The market figure comes from a consultancy report the team has not read; the fourteen per cent has no stated basis. The projection is presented as a single case with no sensitivity analysis.

The executive summary opens with three paragraphs on the burden of musculoskeletal disorders in Europe and reaches the company's proposition on page two. An investor who read only the first page could not say what Vera Health sells.

The risk section lists five risks: competition, regulation, technology, key personnel and funding. No owner is named, no trigger is defined, and each mitigation is one line. The clinical risk that a user follows a programme unsuited to their condition — the risk the advisory board was constituted to address — does not appear.

Much of the plan was drafted with an AI assistant. The prose is fluent and the structure is conventional. Two of the eleven references in the bibliography do not exist; a third exists but says the opposite of what it is cited for. The team was not aware of this until an investor's analyst checked.

The venture has strong evidence it is not using: eleven corporate clients renewed, average programme completion is well above the sector norm, and one client has offered a case study with named outcomes. None of this appears before page nine.

Your task

  1. Rewrite the opening of the executive summary using the Minto Pyramid Principle. What is the governing answer?
  2. Replace the top-down projection with a bottom-up structure. Which drivers must Vera Health be able to defend?
  3. Rebuild the risk matrix with venture-specific risks, owners and triggers. Where does the clinical risk sit?
  4. Two references do not exist. What went wrong in the process, and what does the Prompt-to-Plan Workflow require at each pass?
  5. Write the AI Use Declaration this plan should have carried, using the protocol from Slibrary 8.
Bring written answers to questions 1 and 2; the remaining questions are worked in the session.

Vera Health is a teaching case constructed for MENTR101. The situation is composite; no real company is described.
