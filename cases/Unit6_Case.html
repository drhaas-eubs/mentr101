Baumfeld Coffee: Who Owns the Name?

A Hamburg roastery discovers what it did not register
©️ Dr. Hildegard Haas · Genedia AG Education
Case Study

Baumfeld Coffee roasts and sells single-origin coffee to offices and cafés across northern Germany. Four years old, €1.4 million in revenue, a strong brand built almost entirely through packaging design and a distinctive name.

Three weeks ago the founders received a letter. A Dutch beverage company registered the word mark BAUMFELD as an EU trade mark two years ago in classes 30 and 43. Baumfeld Coffee has never registered anything. It owns the domain, the social handles and a logo drawn by a designer whose contract does not mention the transfer of rights.

The founders had assumed that using the name first protected them. In some circumstances prior use creates rights; establishing them requires evidence they have not systematically kept, and the process would consume money they had planned to spend on a new roaster.

There is a second exposure. The roastery's proprietary element is not the name but a profiling process that predicts roast curves from green bean data — built in-house, never patented, never documented as a trade secret, and described in detail in a trade magazine interview the founder gave last spring. Two employees who worked on it have since left.

A third issue surfaced during the same review. The company's café loyalty app collects names, email addresses and purchase histories from 11,000 customers and feeds them into a recommendation model. No lawful basis was ever documented. The privacy notice was copied from another company's website.

The founders now face a decision with a deadline: contest, rebrand, or negotiate a coexistence agreement — while deciding what to do about the process, the departed employees and the app.

Your task

  1. Map every asset in this case onto the IP Protection Matrix. Which regime should each have used, and what destroyed the option?
  2. Could the roast profiling process still be patented? Apply the Patentability Filter and be specific about what the interview did.
  3. What must the founders have in place for the process to qualify as a trade secret under the EU directive?
  4. Assess the loyalty app against the GDPR lawful basis framework. What must happen before the next marketing email?
  5. Contest, rebrand or coexist. Recommend one, with the cost, the risk and the decision trigger you would set.
Bring written answers to questions 1 and 2; the remaining questions are worked in the session.

Baumfeld Coffee is a teaching case constructed for MENTR101. The situation is composite; no real company is described.
