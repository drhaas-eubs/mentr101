Solvia: The Cheapest Click in Europe

A Barcelona SaaS venture buys growth it cannot keep
©️ Dr. Hildegard Haas · Genedia AG Education
Case Study
The cheapest customer is the one who stays, not the one who clicks.Core message after Bostelaar · Intended learning outcomes 2, 3

Solvia sells scheduling software to independent veterinary clinics. In nine months it has grown from 90 to 1,400 registered accounts, on a marketing budget of €38,000. The founders are proud of a cost per signup of €27, which they present at every opportunity.

The rest of the funnel tells a different story. Of the 1,400 registered accounts, 340 completed setup, 190 imported a client list, and 96 are still active after ninety days. Monthly revenue is €4,800. Cost per active customer, correctly computed, is roughly €400 against a contribution margin of €31 per month.

The content strategy is entirely AI-generated: nine articles a week, published automatically, targeting long-tail search terms. Traffic has grown steadily. So has the volume of signups from clinics in countries where Solvia does not support the local invoicing rules and cannot serve the customer at all.

Personalisation was added in the spring. The product now emails users based on their in-app behaviour and, in one campaign, on the client records they imported. Nobody has established a lawful basis for using imported client data, which belongs to the clinics' own customers, not to Solvia.

One mechanic is under discussion for the next release: a streak counter that resets if the clinic does not open the app for two days, with a notification at each stage. The growth advisor is enthusiastic. The head of customer success has asked whether it helps the clinic or only the metric.

The founders have four months of budget left and a board meeting in three weeks, where they intend to present the €27 figure.

Your task

  1. Rebuild Solvia's metrics using AARRR. Which stage is the actual bottleneck, and what does the €27 figure conceal?
  2. Compute payback and LTV:CAC at the real cost per active customer. What does this say about the business model?
  3. The content strategy attracts users Solvia cannot serve. Apply STP: what has gone wrong in targeting?
  4. Assess the personalisation campaign against the GDPR lawful basis framework. What must stop immediately?
  5. Apply the Hooked model and its manipulation matrix to the proposed streak counter. Recommend a decision and defend it.
Bring written answers to questions 1 and 2; the remaining questions are worked in the session.

Solvia is a teaching case constructed for MENTR101. The situation is composite; no real company is described.
