Fermata: Crossing the Chasm in Nine Months

A Copenhagen venture with enthusiastic early adopters and a stalling pipeline
©️ Dr. Hildegard Haas · Genedia AG Education
Case Study
Scaling is a decision you make deliberately, not a reward you receive for growing.Core message after Bostelaar · Intended learning outcomes 1, 2, 3

Fermata sells rehearsal and scheduling software to independent music schools. Its first forty customers arrived through the founder's own network of music educators, who loved the product, tolerated its rough edges and shaped its roadmap through direct requests.

In the last two quarters growth has stalled. The pipeline is full of larger, more conventional schools that ask questions the founders find tedious: references from schools like theirs, data protection documentation, integration with the accounting package they already use, a service level commitment, and a named support contact. The early adopters asked for none of this.

The team has responded by adding features. Nineteen shipped in six months, most requested by existing enthusiastic customers. None addressed the questions the stalled pipeline is asking.

The organisation has changed too. Fermata has grown from four people to nineteen. The founder still approves every pricing decision, every hire and every roadmap change, and is now the bottleneck in all three. Two senior hires have left within a year, both citing an inability to make decisions in their own area.

Scaling introduced a further issue. The scheduling engine now uses a machine learning component to propose room and teacher allocations. It performs well on the data from the original forty schools and noticeably worse for schools with different timetable structures. There is no monitoring in production, no retraining process, and no documented owner. One school complained after the system repeatedly allocated a teacher outside contracted hours.

Fermata has eleven months of runway and an investor conversation scheduled for the spring.

Your task

  1. Diagnose the stall using Crossing the Chasm. What does the early majority need that Fermata is not providing?
  2. Nineteen features shipped and no chasm crossed. What should the whole-product roadmap have contained instead?
  3. Apply the Greiner Growth Curve. Which crisis is Fermata in, and what structural change does it call for?
  4. Run the AI Scaling Readiness Check on the scheduling engine. Which of the four conditions fail, and what would you fix first?
  5. Apply the Trustworthy AI requirements to the teacher allocation incident. What must exist before the spring investor meeting?
Bring written answers to questions 1 and 2; the remaining questions are worked in the session.

Fermata is a teaching case constructed for MENTR101. The situation is composite; no real company is described.
