Definitions are assessed. A term used loosely in a business plan is a mark lost. Each entry below gives the definition to work from and the source to cite; where a framework has its own gallery card, the same wording is used there.

⌕
AcceleratorUnit 5
A fixed-term, cohort-based programme providing mentoring, a small investment and a demo day in exchange for equity.
Cohen, S. and Hochberg, Y.V. (2014) 'Accelerating startups: the seed accelerator phenomenon', SSRN 2418000.
Affordable LossUnit 1
The maximum amount an entrepreneur is willing to lose on a venture step, used as a decision criterion in place of expected return.
Sarasvathy, S.D. (2001) Academy of Management Review, 26(2).
AI Act (EU)Unit 4
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the European Union's risk-based legal framework for placing AI systems on the market and putting them into service.
European Parliament and Council (2024) Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.
AI DeclarationUnit 4
A statement accompanying submitted work that discloses which AI tools were used, for which tasks, and to what extent the output was revised.
Perkins, M. (2023) Journal of University Teaching & Learning Practice, 20(2).
Angel InvestorUnit 5
A private individual investing personal capital in early-stage ventures, typically before institutional venture capital and often with operating experience.
Feld, B. and Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th edn.
BootstrappingUnit 5
Financing growth from revenue and personal resources rather than external equity, preserving control at the cost of speed.
Winborg, J. and Landstrom, H. (2001) Journal of Business Venturing, 16(3).
Break-Even PointUnit 5
The sales volume at which total revenue equals total cost, calculated as fixed costs divided by contribution margin per unit.
Drury, C. (2018) Management and Cost Accounting. 10th edn.
Burn RateUnit 5
The rate at which a venture consumes cash in excess of receipts, usually expressed per month.
Feld, B. and Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th edn.
Business ModelUnit 3
The rationale describing how an organisation creates, delivers and captures value.
Osterwalder, A. and Pigneur, Y. (2010) Business Model Generation.
Business Model CanvasUnit 3
A one-page visual template describing a business model through nine building blocks.
Osterwalder, A. and Pigneur, Y. (2010) Business Model Generation.
Business PlanUnit 4
A written document setting out a venture's concept, market, strategy, operations, organisation, financial projections and risks.
Aulet, B. (2013) Disciplined Entrepreneurship.
CAC — Customer Acquisition CostUnit 3
The fully loaded cost of acquiring one paying customer, including all sales and marketing expenditure in the period.
Skok, D. (2013) SaaS Metrics 2.0.
Cap TableUnit 5
The record of ownership of a company's securities across founders, employees and investors, before and after each financing round.
Feld, B. and Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th edn.
Cash Flow StatementUnit 5
A financial statement reporting cash movements from operating, investing and financing activities over a period.
Brealey, R.A., Myers, S.C. and Allen, F. (2020) Principles of Corporate Finance. 13th edn.
Causation LogicUnit 1
Decision logic that selects means to achieve a predetermined goal, contrasted with effectuation.
Sarasvathy, S.D. (2001) Academy of Management Review, 26(2).
ChasmUnit 8
The gap in the technology adoption lifecycle between early adopters and the early majority, where many ventures fail.
Moore, G.A. (2014) Crossing the Chasm. 3rd edn.
Cohort AnalysisUnit 8
Analysis grouping customers by the period they joined and tracking their behaviour over time.
Croll, A. and Yoskovitz, B. (2013) Lean Analytics.
Competitive AdvantageUnit 2
The condition in which a firm sustains profits above the industry average through lower cost or differentiation.
Porter, M.E. (1985) Competitive Advantage.
Contribution MarginUnit 5
Revenue less variable costs, representing the amount available to cover fixed costs and profit.
Drury, C. (2018) Management and Cost Accounting. 10th edn.
CopyrightUnit 6
The exclusive right arising automatically on creation of an original work fixed in a tangible form, protecting expression rather than ideas.
World Intellectual Property Organization (2023) WIPO IP Handbook. 3rd edn.
Creative DestructionUnit 1
The process by which innovation dismantles established economic structures and replaces them with new ones.
Schumpeter, J.A. (1942) Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy.
CrowdfundingUnit 5
Raising small amounts from a large number of people through an online platform, typically in reward, equity or lending form.
Mollick, E. (2014) Journal of Business Venturing, 29(1).
Customer DevelopmentUnit 2
A parallel process to product development that tests business model hypotheses with customers before scaling.
Blank, S. and Dorf, B. (2012) The Startup Owner's Manual.
Customer SegmentUnit 3
A distinct group of people or organisations a venture aims to reach and serve, defined by shared needs and behaviours.
Osterwalder, A. and Pigneur, Y. (2010) Business Model Generation.
Data Network EffectUnit 3
The mechanism by which additional usage generates data that improves a product, attracting further usage.
Gregory, R.W. et al. (2021) Academy of Management Review, 46(3).
Design ThinkingUnit 1
A human-centred, iterative approach to innovation combining empathy, ideation and experimentation.
Brown, T. (2008) Harvard Business Review, 86(6), pp. 84-92.
DilutionUnit 5
The reduction in existing shareholders' percentage ownership resulting from the issue of new shares.
Feld, B. and Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th edn.
Disruptive InnovationUnit 2
An innovation that initially serves an overlooked segment with lower performance and then moves upmarket to displace incumbents.
Christensen, C.M. (1997) The Innovator's Dilemma.
Due DiligenceUnit 5
The investigation an investor conducts into a venture's legal, financial, commercial and technical position before investing.
Feld, B. and Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th edn.
EffectuationUnit 1
A decision logic starting from available means and affordable loss, treating the future as co-created rather than predicted.
Sarasvathy, S.D. (2001) Academy of Management Review, 26(2).
EntrepreneurshipUnit 1
The pursuit of opportunity beyond the resources currently controlled, resulting in the creation of new value.
Stevenson, H.H. and Jarillo, J.C. (1990) Strategic Management Journal, 11, pp. 17-27.
Executive SummaryUnit 4
A stand-alone opening section stating the venture's proposition, market, model, financials and ask, written last and read first.
Minto, B. (1987) The Pyramid Principle.
Feasibility AnalysisUnit 1
A structured early assessment of whether a concept is technically, commercially, financially and legally viable.
Barringer, B.R. and Ireland, R.D. (2019) Entrepreneurship: Successfully Launching New Ventures. 6th edn.
FreemiumUnit 3
A revenue model offering a free tier to build reach and converting a proportion of users to a paid tier.
Osterwalder, A. and Pigneur, Y. (2010) Business Model Generation.
GDPRUnit 6
Regulation (EU) 2016/679, which governs the processing of personal data of individuals in the European Union.
European Parliament and Council (2016) Regulation (EU) 2016/679.
Generative AIUnit 1
AI systems that produce new content — text, image, audio, code — from learned statistical patterns in training data.
Bommasani, R. et al. (2021) On the Opportunities and Risks of Foundation Models. Stanford CRFM.
Go-to-Market StrategyUnit 8
The plan defining which segment is targeted first, through which channels, with what offer, message and pricing.
Kawasaki, G. (2015) The Art of the Start 2.0.
HallucinationUnit 2
Fluent but factually unsupported output generated by a language model, presented with the same confidence as accurate output.
Ji, Z. et al. (2023) ACM Computing Surveys, 55(12).
Intellectual PropertyUnit 6
Legally protected creations of the mind, including patents, trade marks, copyright, design rights and trade secrets.
World Intellectual Property Organization (2023) WIPO IP Handbook. 3rd edn.
Jobs to Be DoneUnit 2
The progress a customer is trying to make in a given circumstance, for which they hire a product or service.
Christensen, C.M. et al. (2016) Competing Against Luck.
KPI — Key Performance IndicatorUnit 8
A quantified measure of progress against a defined objective, selected because it can be influenced by decisions.
Croll, A. and Yoskovitz, B. (2013) Lean Analytics.
Lean Start-upUnit 8
A method of venture building based on validated learning through rapid build-measure-learn cycles.
Ries, E. (2011) The Lean Startup.
Liquidation PreferenceUnit 5
A term giving investors the right to receive a defined amount before other shareholders in an exit or liquidation.
Feld, B. and Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th edn.
LTV — Lifetime ValueUnit 3
The total contribution margin expected from a customer over the duration of the relationship.
Skok, D. (2013) SaaS Metrics 2.0.
Market SegmentationUnit 7
The division of a heterogeneous market into groups with distinct needs, characteristics or behaviours.
Kotler, P. and Keller, K.L. (2016) Marketing Management. 15th edn.
Market ValidationUnit 2
The collection of evidence from real customers demonstrating that a defined problem exists and that the proposed solution is wanted.
Blank, S. and Dorf, B. (2012) The Startup Owner's Manual.
MVP — Minimum Viable ProductUnit 8
The smallest product capable of testing a specific hypothesis with real users and generating validated learning.
Ries, E. (2011) The Lean Startup.
Net Promoter ScoreUnit 7
A loyalty metric derived from the likelihood that a customer would recommend the offer to others.
Reichheld, F.F. (2003) Harvard Business Review, 81(12), pp. 46-54.
North Star MetricUnit 8
The single measure that best captures the value a product delivers to its customers, used to align teams.
Croll, A. and Yoskovitz, B. (2013) Lean Analytics.
OKRUnit 4
A goal-setting method pairing a qualitative objective with a small number of measurable key results.
Doerr, J. (2018) Measure What Matters.
Opportunity RecognitionUnit 1
The cognitive process of identifying a viable relationship between an unmet need and a feasible means of meeting it.
Ardichvili, A., Cardozo, R. and Ray, S. (2003) Journal of Business Venturing, 18(1), pp. 105-123.
PatentUnit 6
An exclusive right granted for a new, inventive and industrially applicable technical invention, in exchange for public disclosure.
European Patent Office (2023) European Patent Convention, Articles 52-57.
PivotUnit 8
A structured change of one element of the business model while retaining the learning gained so far.
Ries, E. (2011) The Lean Startup.
PositioningUnit 7
The distinct place an offer occupies in the mind of the target customer relative to alternatives.
Ries, A. and Trout, J. (2001) Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind.
Problem-Solution FitUnit 2
Evidence that a clearly defined customer problem exists and that the proposed solution addresses it.
Maurya, A. (2012) Running Lean. 2nd edn.
Product-Market FitUnit 8
The condition in which a product satisfies strong market demand, typically evidenced by retention and organic growth.
Blank, S. and Dorf, B. (2012) The Startup Owner's Manual.
Prompt EngineeringUnit 1
The practice of designing instructions, examples and constraints to obtain reliable output from a generative model.
Wei, J. et al. (2022) Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 35.
PrototypeUnit 1
A tangible, deliberately incomplete representation of an idea, built to test a specific assumption.
Brown, T. (2008) Harvard Business Review, 86(6).
Revenue StreamUnit 3
The cash a venture generates from each customer segment, defined by pricing mechanism and payment pattern.
Osterwalder, A. and Pigneur, Y. (2010) Business Model Generation.
Risk AppetiteUnit 4
The amount and type of risk an organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives.
International Organization for Standardization (2018) ISO 31000:2018.
RunwayUnit 5
The number of months a venture can operate at its current net burn before cash is exhausted.
Feld, B. and Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th edn.
ScalabilityUnit 8
The capacity of a business model to increase revenue substantially faster than the costs required to deliver it.
Iansiti, M. and Lakhani, K.R. (2020) Competing in the Age of AI.
Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)Unit 2
The share of the serviceable market a venture can realistically capture within its planning horizon.
Mullins, J.W. (2013) The New Business Road Test. 4th edn.
Stage-GateUnit 3
A product development process in which work passes through defined stages separated by go/no-go decision gates.
Cooper, R.G. (2017) Winning at New Products. 5th edn.
Trade MarkUnit 6
A sign distinguishing the goods or services of one undertaking from those of others, protected within registered classes.
World Intellectual Property Organization (2023) Nice Classification. 12th edn.
Trade SecretUnit 6
Commercially valuable information that is secret and subject to reasonable steps to keep it secret.
European Parliament and Council (2016) Directive (EU) 2016/943.
Unique Selling PropositionUnit 2
The specific, defensible reason a target customer should choose this offer over the available alternatives.
Ries, A. and Trout, J. (2001) Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind.
Unit EconomicsUnit 3
The revenue and cost associated with a single unit of the business model, typically one customer or one transaction.
Skok, D. (2013) SaaS Metrics 2.0.
Validated LearningUnit 8
Knowledge about the business model demonstrated by empirical evidence from real customer behaviour.
Ries, E. (2011) The Lean Startup.
Value PropositionUnit 3
The bundle of products and services that creates value for a specific customer segment by relieving pains or creating gains.
Osterwalder, A. et al. (2014) Value Proposition Design.
Venture CapitalUnit 5
Professionally managed equity investment in high-growth-potential ventures in exchange for a minority stake and governance rights.
Feld, B. and Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th edn.
Working CapitalUnit 5
Current assets less current liabilities: the short-term capital tied up in operating the business.
Brealey, R.A., Myers, S.C. and Allen, F. (2020) Principles of Corporate Finance. 13th edn.
