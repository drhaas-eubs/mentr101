Why Good Ideas Are Not Opportunities

The difference between something that could work and something you can pursue
©️ Dr. Hildegard Haas · Genedia AG Education
Pre-Reading
Key framework
NABC Idea Screen · Effectuation

Every entrepreneurship course begins with the same misunderstanding: that the scarce resource is ideas. It is not. Ideas are abundant, cheap, and — since generative AI became ordinary — effectively unlimited. What remains scarce is the capacity to tell which idea is an opportunity for a specific team, at a specific moment, with specific means.

Stevenson and Jarillo (1990) defined entrepreneurship as the pursuit of opportunity beyond the resources currently controlled. Read carefully, the definition contains a warning: an opportunity is relational. It exists in the space between a market condition and a particular actor's means. The same market gap can be an opportunity for one team and a fantasy for another, and no amount of enthusiasm converts the second into the first.

This is where the screening frameworks earn their place. The NABC structure developed at SRI International forces four statements that an idea alone cannot produce: whose need, addressed how, at what benefit per cost, against which competition — including the competition of the customer changing nothing. Ideas survive conversation; NABC statements do not survive it unless evidence exists behind them.

Sarasvathy's (2001) study of expert entrepreneurs adds the second half of the argument. Her subjects did not begin with a goal and search for means. They began with three questions — who I am, what I know, whom I know — and allowed the goal to emerge from what those means made possible. Where the future cannot be predicted, this is not a lesser method; it is the only one that produces action rather than analysis.

The practical consequence for this course is a sequence. Generate widely, including with AI. Screen ruthlessly, using a structure rather than a preference. Then check the surviving idea against your actual means and the loss you can afford. An idea that passes all three is an opportunity. An idea that passes only the first is a conversation.

One caution about the generation step. AI will produce plausible market sizes, competitor lists and customer segments for any idea you give it, including ideas that make no sense. Fluency is not evidence. Every figure you carry from the generation step into the screening step must be traceable to a source you have opened yourself.

An idea becomes an opportunity when it meets a stated need, uses means you actually control, and survives a screen you did not design to let it pass.

Questions to bring to the session

  1. Which of your own three means — who you are, what you know, whom you know — is strongest, and what does it make possible?
  2. Name an idea you have abandoned. Was it a weak idea, or a weak opportunity for you specifically?
  3. What evidence would have to appear for you to stop pursuing your current idea?
