Cash Is a Date, Not a Number

Why profitable ventures die and forecasts that convince nobody
©️ Dr. Hildegard Haas · Genedia AG Education
Pre-Reading
Key framework
Three-Statement Model · Burn Rate & Runway

The most common cause of death in growing ventures is not weak demand. It is the gap between when money leaves and when money arrives. A business can be profitable on every unit it sells and still fail, because profit is an accounting statement about a period and cash is a fact about a date.

The three-statement model exists to make that gap visible. The income statement tells you whether the business earns; the balance sheet tells you what it is owed and what it owes; the cash flow statement tells you whether it survives. Founders who build only the first are not modelling a business, they are modelling a hope. Working capital — inventory, receivables, payables — is where the cash actually sits, and it appears in none of the numbers most early plans present.

Burn rate and runway convert the model into the only question that matters before profitability: how many months are left, and what date does the raise have to close. A venture with six months of runway is already in a difficult negotiation, because the counterparty can see the calendar too.

Forecasting method decides whether anyone believes the numbers. A top-down projection — a market size multiplied by an assumed share — cannot be challenged driver by driver, which is precisely why investors discount it. A bottom-up forecast built from channels, capacity, conversion and price can be attacked, and surviving that attack is what credibility means.

Two disciplines close the unit. First, sensitivity: find the two assumptions that dominate the outcome and show what happens when they move. A single-case projection tells the reader you have not looked. Second, confidentiality: financial data, customer lists and pricing are not material for public AI services. AI is useful for building and checking model structure; the inputs stay yours, and personal data requires a lawful basis before it is processed at all.

Bring your own numbers to the session. We will break them, which is cheaper here than in Week 10.

Model the cash, not just the profit; forecast from drivers you can defend; and know the date, not just the number.

Questions to bring to the session

  1. On what date does your venture run out of money, and what is the cheapest action that moves it?
  2. Which two assumptions dominate your forecast, and how confident are you in each?
  3. What data in your model would you refuse to paste into a public AI tool, and why?
