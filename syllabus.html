1. Course description

The course introduces students to the various stages of building successful and innovative business ventures. It applies key theoretical concepts drawn from current studies and from entrepreneurial management practice. Students examine and critically analyse the stages of the entrepreneurial cycle, from the first idea to the introduction of a final product or service to market. The course provides a broader perspective on entrepreneurship by examining entrepreneurial behaviours and capabilities for new ventures, and develops core competencies in both academic research and practical entrepreneurial management, so that students build an applied understanding of how ideas transform into value.

2. Intended learning outcomes

On successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Systematically analyse current and future business opportunities in terms of entrepreneurial behaviours and capability creation.
  2. Interpret trends, behaviours and changes in various market environments.
  3. Analyse the stages of the entrepreneurial cycle.
  4. Demonstrate an understanding of the importance of research and careful planning when developing innovative business ventures.

Teaching notes

Pedagogical approach

EU Business School's approach is hands-on and experiential. Small group sizes allow practical learning activities in which students apply knowledge and interact directly with faculty and peers. Sessions combine theory, practical cases, graphic material and in-class pitching, with scaffolded activities that develop critical thinking through application, debate and questioning.

Specific considerations for this course

  • Updated tools and terminology are used throughout: problem-solution fit, Lean Start-up, Value Proposition Canvas.
  • Modern case examples are drawn from contemporary ventures rather than legacy textbook cases.
  • Creativity and ideation tools overlapping with other core courses are treated at application level here, not re-taught from first principles.
  • Assessment tasks are aligned to the intended learning outcomes, particularly on customer discovery and MVP validation.
  • AI content is integrated into every unit as a second Slibrary rather than taught as a separate module.
