Core message · Unit 3
A working product is not a business; the model is how the value you create comes back as money.
  • A business model states who pays, for what, on what schedule, and at what cost to serve them.
  • Every block of it is a hypothesis until evidence is attached to it.
  • Unit economics, not enthusiasm, decide whether growth creates or destroys value.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
1Systematically analyse current and future business opportunities in terms of entrepreneurial behaviours and capability creation.
3Analyse the stages of the entrepreneurial cycle.
Core message distilled after Bostelaar (2024), Clarify Your Core Message: decompose, prioritise, three sentences, one sentence.

Driving question

Who pays, for what, and why does the money keep coming?

Unit content (course outline, section 4)

  • Business Model Canvas: introduction to the canvas, defining value propositions, and key components
  • Revenue Streams: identifying potential revenue models, pricing strategies, and revenue projections
  • Cost Structure: understanding fixed and variable costs, cost management, and breakeven analysis

AI integration in this unit

AI changes both sides of the canvas: it enables new value propositions and data-driven cost structures, and it adds a cost stack of its own. Students model AI as a business-model component, not as a feature.

In-class activities

Two activities you run with the frameworks on this page. Each is timed, each ends in something you hand in, and each feeds the assessment. The full seven-phase session plan is in the session activities for this unit.

A1
Read the Canvas Aloud Until It Breaks
25 min

Frameworks: Business Model Canvas (sheet 22) · Revenue Model Taxonomy (sheet 25)  ·  Working format: Teams

  1. Ten minutes: draw all nine blocks on one sheet. Bullets only, no paragraphs, no full sentences.
  2. Five minutes: read the canvas aloud as a single sentence — for this segment, we solve this, delivered this way, paid for like this, at this cost.
  3. Five minutes: mark on the canvas the exact place where the sentence broke. Do not repair it yet.
  4. Five minutes: name your revenue pattern from the taxonomy and state one consequence out loud: when the cash actually arrives.

You hand in: A nine-block canvas with the break marked, and a named revenue pattern.

Watch out: A canvas completed as a form produces nine unrelated lists. The read-aloud test is what turns it back into a model.

A2
Cost the Iceberg Before You Claim the Margin
20 min

Frameworks: AI Cost Iceberg (sheet 30) · Unit Economics: CAC, LTV, Payback (sheet 26)  ·  Working format: Teams

  1. Five minutes: place AI in exactly one block of your canvas — value proposition, key activity, key resource or cost structure. One block only, and defend the choice.
  2. Ten minutes: cost every layer of the iceberg: licences, data work, integration, monitoring, human review. Any layer you enter as zero needs a written justification beside it.
  3. Five minutes: recompute your contribution margin per customer with those costs included. State whether the model still works.

You hand in: An AI cost line with five layers priced, and a revised contribution margin.

Watch out: Monitoring and human review are the layers teams set to zero. Those are the two that do not go away as you scale.
