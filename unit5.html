Core message · Unit 5
Cash is a date, not a number — know the date, and know what moves it.
  • Profit is a statement about a period; cash is a fact about a day.
  • Ventures fail in the gap between when money leaves and when it arrives.
  • A forecast is credible only when every driver behind it can be defended on its own.

In-class activities

Two activities you run with the frameworks on this page. Each is timed, each ends in something you hand in, and each feeds the assessment. The full seven-phase session plan is in the session activities for this unit.

A1
Assumptions Before Statements
25 min

Frameworks: Three-Statement Model (sheet 42) · Bottom-Up vs Top-Down Forecasting (sheet 45)  ·  Working format: Teams

  1. Ten minutes: build one assumptions sheet before you open any statement — price, volume driver, conversion rate, cost per unit, headcount, timing.
  2. Five minutes: beside every driver write a source, or the word ASSUMED in capitals. There is no third option.
  3. Five minutes: build your volume line bottom-up from a capacity or activity driver. If you already had a top-down figure, put the two side by side.
  4. Five minutes: explain the gap between the two numbers out loud. The explanation, not the number, is what you will be marked on.

You hand in: An assumptions sheet in which every driver is sourced or flagged, and two volume forecasts.

Watch out: Hard-coded numbers inside formulas are the defect that cannot be unpicked later. Check for them now, while the model is small.

A2
Find the Date the Money Runs Out
20 min

Frameworks: Burn Rate & Runway (sheet 43) · Base-Best-Worst Sensitivity (sheet 46)  ·  Working format: Teams

  1. Five minutes: compute monthly net burn and state the date your venture runs out of cash. Write the date down.
  2. Five minutes: vary one driver at a time — price, conversion, cost — and record how far each moves that date. One driver at a time or the exercise means nothing.
  3. Five minutes: name the two drivers that dominate. Those two are your management dashboard for the next year.
  4. Five minutes: build the worst case from those two and confirm the venture still exists in it. Survivable is a fact, not a mood.

You hand in: A runway date, the two dominant drivers, and a survivable worst case.

Watch out: A worst case that is merely unpleasant has not been stressed. Push each dominant driver until the date moves inside six months.
